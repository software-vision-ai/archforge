# archforge-er

**Purpose.** The ER notation: a schema of entities with ordered attributes, and relationships with
a cardinality at each end, as a metamodel over the MOF core (`er.xmi`, generated by `cargo xtask
generate er`) with the rules and commands written here. A schema is a member of the project's Model
(the shared core); comments on ER elements are the core's; ER diagrams are the diagram metamodel's
(`archforge-diagram`), with the ER policy in `ErNotation`, which the project's view asks about ER's
diagrams and elements: an `er` diagram is of a schema (always, and
only while the project has its Model), shows that schema's entities as nodes (160 by 100 by default)
and relationships as edges, whose labels show the name, the two cardinalities and the two roles.

**Layer.** 7, the notations' layer, above the MOF core (4), the shared core (5) and the diagram
metamodel (6); pure, builds for the portable target. Storage comes with the generated tables;
the canvas later.

**Binding rules.** Every edit is a registered command with documented parameters, refused centrally
when unknown or out of range and refused by the handler, naming the key, when an id is not an element
of the expected kind. Ids in parameters are the elements' UUIDs. The MOF core enforces types,
bounds, containment and the derived union `ownedElement`; the ER rules (`rules`) add: names plain, at
most 128 characters and unique ignoring case among a schema's entities and an entity's attributes;
a type at most 64 characters; a relationship's ends are entities of its schema.

Keys are constraints. An entity has at most one primary key and any number of unique constraints,
each with its own name and its own column order: at least one and at most 32 of the entity's
columns, none twice; a primary key's columns are never nullable. The column editor's checkboxes are
shortcuts: Primary key adds the column to the end of the primary key (making one when there is
none), Unique makes a unique constraint over that column alone. A unique constraint says whether
rows with nulls clash (`nullsDistinct`).

A foreign key points at a key, the primary key or any unique constraint (so also a composite
alternate key), of an entity in the same schema, and lists its own columns matched to the key's by
position: as many, none twice, of its own entity, each typed as its target (ignoring case, as SQL
does). Set null needs nullable columns; its relationship, when set, joins the same two entities; an
identifying relationship's key columns are part of the child's primary key. It has a match type
(simple, full, partial) and a deferrability (not deferrable, initially immediate, initially
deferred). Constraint names, of keys and foreign keys, are unique in a schema ignoring case. A key
that a foreign key points at keeps its columns: changing them is refused until the foreign key is
changed or removed. A mapping table is an entity of kind associative with at least two foreign keys
inside its primary key.

Each end of a relationship may carry a role, such as parent and child, or billing and shipping. The
role at the end a foreign key points to names its columns in place of the entity's name
(`<role>_<column>`), so Order with two relationships to Address gets `billing_address_id` and
`shipping_address_id`, and Part with a parent gets `parent_part_id`. A relationship of an entity with
itself may bring its foreign key once the end it points to has a role. Resolving a many-to-many
names the mapping table's columns by the roles and gives the roles to its two new relationships;
without roles, a many-to-many of an entity with itself resolves into two relationships the
notice below points out.
Relationships between the same two entities that no role tells apart are noticed
(`rules::SAME_ENDS`), not refused; roles are names (plain, at most 128 characters) and renaming one
does not rename the columns it named.

Edits that would break a key are refused and name their parameter (`columns`, `key`, `references`,
`on_delete`, `relationship`, `points_to`, `name`, or `primary_key`, `unique` and `identifying` for
the edits that would break one). A relationship that brings its foreign key points at the parent's
primary key, or at the key `points_to` names; the key columns it adds are named `<role>_<column>`
when the end it points to has a role, else `<parent>_<column>` (with `_2`, `_3`, … when taken), typed like their targets, and for a one-to-one a unique constraint
covers them together unless they are the child's whole primary key, which is unique already. The
edit commands for keys and foreign keys (`set`, `set-options`) take every field, so nothing the
caller leaves out is reset. `er.foreign-key.set-key` points a foreign key at another key, with given
columns or with new ones. A many-to-many relationship is allowed and noticed (`rules::notices`,
`er.schema.get`'s `notices`): it becomes a table only through a mapping table, which
`er.relationship.resolve` creates in one step (the associative entity with the key columns of both
ends, a foreign key to each, two identifying one-to-many relationships, and on every diagram the
mapping table midway on the old line, or beside the entity for a relationship with itself); the
default name `<from>_<to>` steps aside with `_2`, a given name that is taken is refused.

Removing an ER element takes everything it contains, the keys and foreign keys that depend on any of
it, the diagrams of it, the nodes and edges that show any of them and the trace links on any of
them, in one step (`commands::remove_element`). As in SQL, a key goes whole with any of its columns,
and the foreign keys onto it go with it. A mapping table that loses a key becomes a regular entity,
and an identifying relationship that loses its key, or whose key leaves the primary key, a
non-identifying one; an entity's removal takes the relationships that reach it too
(`commands::remove_entity`).

Checks and indexes (slice S3) belong to an entity: a `CheckConstraint` holds an SQL expression every
row holds and an optional name; a `TableIndex` (named so because `Index` would clash with the
generated package index) holds its columns in order, each an `IndexColumn` with an attribute of the
entity or an expression, a sort direction and a nulls order, plus unique, an optional partial-index
condition (where) and an access method as free text. Their names share the schema's constraint
namespace with keys and foreign keys; an unnamed one shows a generated name (`rules::display_name`:
`ck_<table>_<n>`, `ix_<table>_<columns>`). Removing an attribute removes the index columns on it,
and an index left without columns (`commands::index_dependents`); a check's text is not parsed, so
column references inside it are not checked.

Relationships and keys agree (slice S4). A relationship's parent end drives the nullability of its
foreign key's columns (`er.relationship.set-ends`: zero-or-one makes them nullable, exactly-one not
null, a column in the child's primary key stays not null), and making such a column nullable or not
moves the parent end the same way; a one-or-more child end is a notice (`rules::ONE_OR_MORE`), since
no key enforces it. A key that grows grows the foreign keys onto it: each gets a matching new
column, named after the role and typed like the key column; a key that shrinks takes the follower
columns that nothing else holds, and a key that reorders reorders them. A column a relationship or
foreign key made follows the name and type of the column it points at (`Attribute.linkedTo`,
`rules::linked_to`, `linked_from`) until the user edits it, which clears the link, or runs
`er.attribute.unlink`; `er.foreign-key.set-key` without columns removes the old followers it no
longer needs. A pure mapping table, one that holds exactly the columns of its two foreign keys, all
in its primary key, and no other key, check or index (`rules::collapse_problem`), collapses back into
the many-to-many relationship it stood for (`er.relationship.collapse`, `commands::collapse`),
zero-or-more at both ends since the table keeps no record of the old line, drawn between the two
parents on every diagram where the table was. Identifying relationships never form a cycle: the
switch, `er.foreign-key.add` and `set-key` refuse one (`rules::identifying_cycle`), and growing
keys has a depth budget behind that.

Domains and enum types (slice S5) are owned by a schema and share its one name namespace with the
entities, as SQL does. A `Domain` is built on a base type with its length, precision or scale, a
default, Not null and check expressions; an `EnumType` holds its literals as an ordered list of
strings. A column's type is its own type name, or a domain, or an enum type, exactly one of the
three (`er.attribute.add` / `set-type` take `domain` or `enum` in place of `type`); `rules::type_of`
and `size_of` read through the domain, so every rule on types (key pairs, identity columns) holds
for typed columns, and `type_display` shows the type's name. An enum column's default is one of the
literals; a column on a domain takes no own size, default or collation. A type in use cannot be
removed (`er.domain.remove`, `er.enum.remove` name the columns); changing one re-checks its columns.

Subtypes (slice S6): a `Generalization` owned by the schema says a subtype is a kind of its
supertype, one element per subtype, so a diagram draws each as one line from the subtype to the
supertype. The facts of the whole set live once on the supertype: complete, disjoint, the
discriminator column and the mapping (table per type, single table, table per concrete type).
Under table per type, a supertype with a primary key gives each subtype an identifying one to
zero-or-one key relationship with follower columns named like its key columns
(`commands::ensure_subtype_key`); the generalization owns that key, which goes with it or with a
mapping change and cannot be removed on its own; a key that appears later reaches the subtypes then.
A table has one supertype and generalizations never close a circle (`rules::generalization_problem`).

Several schemas, sequences and views (slice S7): one model is one database with several schemas.
A foreign key and a relationship may join tables of two schemas; the relationship belongs to its
child's schema, as its foreign key does, and a many-to-many one resolves into a mapping table of
that schema. Generalizations stay inside one schema. A schema's diagram may show tables of other
schemas (`ErNotation::may_show`), and removing a schema takes the relationships of other schemas that
reach into it (`commands::remove_schema`). A `Sequence` (start, increment never 0, minimum and
maximum as whole numbers kept as text, cycle, owned by a column of its schema) and a `View` (its
SQL query as text, not parsed, materialized) are owned by a schema; tables, domains, enum types,
sequences and views share one name namespace per schema. A sequence is owned by a whole-number
column of its schema and forgets it when the column goes. A column may take a domain or enum type
of another schema, as a key column following its target does; when that schema goes, the column
keeps the domain's base type and size, or text for an enum type.

**The metaclasses.** `Schema` (entity, relationship, domain, enumType, generalization, sequence, view), `Entity` (attribute, kind, primaryKey,
uniqueConstraint, foreignKey, checkConstraint, tableIndex, subtypesComplete, subtypesDisjoint, discriminator, subtypeMapping), `Attribute` (type with length, precision and scale, nullable, default, generation with an expression, collation, linkedTo, domain, enumType), `Relationship` (name, from, to,
fromEnd, toEnd, fromRole, toRole, identifying), the abstract `Key` (name, column) with `PrimaryKey` and
`UniqueConstraint` (nullsDistinct), `ForeignKey` (name, references a Key, column, onDelete,
onUpdate, relationship, match, deferrable) and the enumerations `Cardinality` (zeroOrOne,
exactlyOne, zeroOrMore, oneOrMore), `EntityKind` (regular, associative), `ReferentialAction`
(noAction, restrict, cascade, setNull, setDefault), `MatchType` (simple, full, partial) and
`Deferrability` (notDeferrable, initiallyImmediate, initiallyDeferred); `CheckConstraint` (name,
expression), `TableIndex` (name, unique, column, where, method), `IndexColumn` (attribute or
expression, direction, nulls) with `SortDirection` (ascending, descending) and `NullsOrder` (default,
first, last); `Domain` (name, baseType, length, precision, scale, default, notNull, check), `DomainCheck`
(name, expression), `EnumType` (name, literal), `Generalization` (supertype, subtype, keyRelationship) with
`SubtypeMapping` (tablePerType, singleTable, tablePerConcreteType); `Sequence` (name, start, increment,
minimum, maximum, cycle, ownedBy) and `View` (name, query, materialized).

Commands: `er.schema.{add, rename, remove}`, `er.entity.{add, rename, remove, set-kind}`,
`er.attribute.{add, rename, set-type, set-flags, move, remove}`, `er.relationship.{add, set-ends,
set-roles, rename, remove, set-identifying, resolve}` (`add` takes `create_foreign_key`,
`identifying`, `points_to`, `from_role` and `to_role`), `er.primary-key.{set, remove}`, `er.unique.{add, set, remove}`,
`er.foreign-key.{add, set-actions, set-options, set-key, rename, remove}`,
`er.sequence.{add, set, remove}`, `er.view.{add, set, remove}`,
the queries `er.schema.{list, get}` and `er.entity.{list, get}`;
the diagram commands (`diagram.*`, `view.open-diagram`) and the shared `view.select`,
`element.remove` and `element.describe` come from `archforge-diagram`; the last two reach ER's
`commands::remove_elements` and `commands::describe_element` through `ErNotation`.

**Tests.** `tests/er.rs`: the registry and the metamodel; every command's round trip on the store;
refusals that name their key and change nothing; removing an entity with a core folder, comment and
link around it, and the mixed delete through the engine; foreign keys (round trips, refusals that
would break a key, each naming its parameter, removal with what they depend on, two-column keys);
new key columns and identifying relationships that would break a key elsewhere; relationships that
bring their foreign key, identifying or not, with names that step aside and uniqueness for one-to-one;
removals that clear `identifying`; keys as named constraints with their own order, composite unique
constraints, the checkbox shortcuts, keys that go whole with a column; foreign keys onto any key,
pointed elsewhere with given or new columns, match and deferrability, keys that stay while a foreign
key points at them; relationships pointed at a unique key; removing a primary key with what depends
on it; roles that name key columns and tell relationships between the same entities apart, an
entity related to itself in its own table and through a mapping table; resolving a many-to-many into a mapping table on a diagram (also an
entity with itself), undo, name collisions, and demotion when a key or an end goes; the sweep with ids of every kind; seeded random sequences (with keys, unique constraints, repointed foreign keys, resolves and type
changes) with the rules checked after each step and undo back to the start.
`tests/diagram.rs`: the ER policy; every diagram command on an ER diagram; refusals the policy
makes; model removals that take their views and undo that brings them back; the open diagram in the
view; the grid; the sweep; random sequences over model and diagram commands together.

**Known lacks.** No indexes, checks or
views. A mapping table cannot yet be collapsed back into a many-to-many relationship.
Attribute types are free text; the PostgreSQL names decide what size a type takes (`rules::type_shape`: a length for `VARCHAR`, `CHAR`, `BIT`, and MySQL's `BINARY` (to 255) and `VARBINARY`, which a project's own ids are; a precision for `TIME`, `TIMESTAMP`, `INTERVAL`; precision and scale for `NUMERIC`, `DECIMAL`), other dialects follow behind a dialect setting (owner, 2026-10-30). Every element takes a `description`, the text DDL writes as its comment.

**Non-goals.** Drawing, storage, the canvas, MCP tools, the class model.
